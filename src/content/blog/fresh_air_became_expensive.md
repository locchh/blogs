Not about tech this time. This one is a thought I can't put down.

I want two things, and I think you want them too. **Convenience**: work nearby, food at midnight, a hospital ten minutes away, people to meet. And **comfort**: quiet, space, clean air, a calm mind. Simple wants. Here is the joke: they refuse to live in the same place.

If I want to feel comfortable — peaceful, mind clean — I have to leave the city. Drive for hours, past the traffic, to where the air is fresh. Stop and look at that sentence. **Fresh air used to be the freest thing on Earth.** What kind of society did we build, where the free things became the hard things to get?

The answer is not a mystery. It's arithmetic. Convenience *works* — that's the problem. Everything that makes the city convenient for me makes it convenient for a thousand other people, and they all come, same as me. Each one takes a slice of the quiet, a lane of the road, a breath of the air. **Convenience is a shared resource, and a shared resource that works becomes a queue.** The city never sells convenience for money alone. It charges comfort on top.

Economists have a name for part of this: [positional goods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Positional_good) — things that lose their value as more people get them. A quiet street is only quiet while few people have found it. A shortcut is only short while it is yours.

The countryside runs the same trade in reverse. It gives the comfort back — the air, the quiet, the sleep — and charges you in convenience: everything is far, everyone is far, the hospital is an hour away. And there is no third village where both are cheap. If such a place appears, a thousand people move there. Then it's a city.

So I've stopped asking how to get both. Wrong question. There is one budget, paid in two currencies, and the only real choice is the exchange rate: how much comfort I will pay to stay near everything, and how often I will drive out of the city to buy a little back.

> **Lesson:** convenience and comfort are not two things to want. They are one bill, and you only choose which currency to pay it in.
